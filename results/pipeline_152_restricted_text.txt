Both treat a participant's recordings as exchangeable across nights and across positions within a night. That is justified under the null of Equation~\eqref{eq:hypothesis}, conditional on the EEG and on the assignment of windows to recordings and participants, if a participant's labels do not depend on the night or the time of night; if labels and EEG both drifted across nights or through a night, exchanging labels across them could create or remove an association. The primary evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:howmuch}) was therefore also tested under two restricted schemes: labels exchanged only among the recordings of one night, and only within consecutive blocks of three recordings of a night ordered by their position in it, which also preserves slow drifts through the night. Of the 492 recordings, 491 share a night with at least one other and 469 share a block, and only those can exchange labels. Under the night restriction the four configurations give $p$ from 0.3509 to 0.7692, and under the block restriction from 0.2020 to 0.6452 (sequential, as in Section~\ref{sec:howmuch}; after Holm's correction 0.8081 or more), so the conclusions of Section~\ref{sec:howmuch} do not depend on exchanges across nights or positions within a night.
